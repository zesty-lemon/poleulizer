EVALUATION & RESULTS  (~5 min, 7 slides)
Numbers from final_report/reports_for_presentation/full_report.txt and
isolated_report.txt. Items marked [outline] come from presentation_outline.txt
(the earlier 690-row run) and were not re-checked against the new reports.


Slide 1: Building ground truth
- Hand-labeled every (pole, frame) pair on US 5 St Johnsbury: which box is
  really the pole, or "no box" / "not visible"
- 763 pole-frames, labeled once against frozen detections
- Same ground truth re-scored against every matcher version since
- Scored as precision / recall / F1 on the "there is a box and it's this one"
  decision, so the many correctly-empty frames don't inflate the score
- A pole_visible flag separates detector misses from matcher misses


Slide 2: Headline results (full route, 763 pole-frames)
  Match precision    62.3%
  Match recall       54.0%
  Match F1           57.8%
  Matcher accuracy   55.4%   (excluding detector misses)
  Detector recall    97.0%   (587 / 605 visible poles boxed)
  Median residual    52.8 px on matched pairs
- Detector almost never misses a pole. The errors are the matcher's.


Slide 3: Where the errors go
  correct match    317   42%
  correct reject    96   13%
  wrong box        130   17%
  missed           140   18%
  false match       62    8%
  detector miss     18    2%
- Wrong box: the right box was in the frame, the matcher picked a neighbor
- Wrong-box errors sit near image center (median |theta| 4.5 deg), not at
  the edges, so a tighter angle gate wouldn't help
- Missed: almost all "no box within the gate" (130), only 10 at the frame edge
- Cause: near the vanishing point a far-ahead right pole and a left-of-road
  pole land on nearly the same pixel column; one frame can't separate them

Slide 5: The crowding problem
- The matcher works one frame at a time: for each catalog pole, predict a
  pixel column, pick the nearest box
- On US 5 a frame rarely holds just one pole
    poles in frame     1     2     3     4
    frames           169   116    83   12      (380 frames)
- Median two poles per frame, up to four, and each pole appears in ~15
  frames as the car approaches
- Poles are ~48 m apart on these roads, so neighbors are the norm  [outline]
- Near the vanishing point, two poles 50 m apart project to nearly the same
  column. The predicted positions overlap, and the nearest box is a coin flip
- This is the "wrong box" and "false match" bucket from slide 3: the right
  box was there, a neighbor's box was closer


Slide 6: Isolating the matcher from the crowd
- Question: how good is the matcher when there is nothing to confuse it with?
- Define an isolated pole-frame: the localization step places exactly one
  catalog pole in that frame
- Computed from the observations alone, no hand labeling, so it runs on any
  route
- Per pole-frame, not per pole: a pole is usually alone when the car is
  close to it and shares its earlier, farther frames with neighbors
- Still a real test: the frame can hold several detector boxes (other
  poles, signs, false positives). Only the competing catalog pole is gone
- US 5: 169 of 698 pole-frames (24%), covering 26 of 52 poles
- Same matcher, same gate, same ground truth. Only the subset changes
  (Speaker note: if the score jumps, the ceiling is crowding, not the
  geometry or the detector. If it doesn't, the matcher itself is the limit.)


Slide 7: The density result
                           n    precision   recall    F1
  Pole alone in frame     176     81.7%     81.1%    81.4%
  All poles               763     62.3%     54.0%    57.8%
- Same matcher, same gate: a pole with no competitor in its frame scores
  24 F1 points higher
- Detector recall on isolated poles: 99.2%
- The ceiling is assignment ambiguity from pole density, not the geometry
  or the detector
- No sparser located object class exists in the data (trees and rocks are
  clustered, bridge piers have no GPS), so the fix has to come from the
  matcher  [outline]
- Next step: use the frames where a pole is alone to anchor its identity,
  then carry that through the crowded frames (temporal consistency)
