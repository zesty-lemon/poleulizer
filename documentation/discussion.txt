SLIDE D1 — Discussion: the method is valid, crowding is the limit
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- When a pole is alone in its frame, the matcher finds the right box 81% of
  the time (F1 81.4%), with 3 to 4 distractor boxes still present.
- Across the full route, F1 drops to 57.8%. The only difference is that most
  frames hold two or three catalog poles.
- Neither the detector (97% recall) nor the geometry is the cause.
- The cause is assignment. Near the vanishing point, neighboring poles land
  about 130 px apart and a one-frame matcher cannot separate them. That is a
  solvable matching problem, not a flaw in the method.

Speaker notes:
  This is the thesis slide; everything after it is evidence for one sentence.
  On the detector: the correct box was present in the frame in every one of
  the 130 wrong-box cases, so a better detector would change nothing. On the
  geometry: when I control for distance, isolated poles beat crowded poles by
  the widest margin at long range, 86% versus 43%. If the geometry were the
  problem, that gap would close with distance, not widen.

SLIDE D2 — Crowding is a gradient, not a switch
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Table (same matcher, same gate, same ground truth):

    catalog poles in frame    pole-frames    precision    recall     F1
    1                              176         81.7%      81.1%    81.4%
    2                              264         66.1%      55.3%    60.2%
    3                              267         47.1%      38.2%    42.2%
    4                               56         34.8%      33.3%    34.0%

- Each additional pole in the frame costs roughly 15 to 20 F1 points.
- Precision is 93% when the pole is within 30 m and under 50% beyond 90 m.
- Physical reason: 50 m of road spacing projects to ~130 px near the vanishing
  point. The gate is 225 px. Column position alone cannot separate them.

Speaker notes:
  The one-pole row is the isolated subset by definition. The point of showing
  all four rows is that the decline is smooth and monotone. Nothing else
  changes between the rows: not the labels, not the detections, not the
  parameters. This is what makes "crowding" a measurement rather than an
  excuse. The four-pole row is only 56 pole-frames, so treat it as noisy.
  (How the table was made: count distinct catalog poles per ground-truth
  frame, tag each pole-frame with that count, score each bucket with the
  same categories evaluate.compare uses.)

SLIDE D3 — The isolated subset is a fair test
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Isolated = exactly one catalog pole localized to the frame. Computed from the
  observations table alone, no hand labeling, so it runs on any route.
- Per pole-frame, not per pole: a pole is usually alone mid-approach and shares
  its farther frames with neighbors. 176 of 763 pole-frames, 28 of 56 poles.
- Only the competing catalog pole is removed. Isolated frames still average
  3.4 detector boxes (left-of-road poles, unlabeled poles, false positives).
- Not a distance effect: isolated and crowded pole-frames sit at similar
  ranges (71 m vs 78 m median), and the gap persists when range is matched.
- The residual 18% error when alone is an unlabeled pole next to the catalog
  pole, never a left-side box. That is the annotation limit, not the matcher.

Speaker notes:
  This slide pre-empts "you picked the easy frames." Three facts: the
  distractors are still there, the distances are the same, and nothing else
  changed. Keep the definition bullets from the old "Refocusing the problem"
  slide; the counts here (176 / 763 / 28 / 56) match the Results tables. The
  old slide quoted 169 / 698 / 26 / 52, which came from the other export of
  the route and should not be mixed with the 763-row results.

SLIDE D4 — Beyond utility poles
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Signs, bridge piers, and culverts are sparse in the frame on almost every
  road. Poles on rural two-lane corridors are the hard case.
- None of those objects carry a GPS point in this dataset, so they could not be
  tested directly, and time did not allow another round of annotation.
- Isolated poles are the stand-in: same pipeline, same distractor boxes, no
  competing catalog object. F1 81.4%.
- The pipeline is object-agnostic. Switching targets changes one annotation
  field and one detector prompt; no new machinery.

Speaker notes:
  The only object-specific inputs are the labeled lat/lon in the XML and the
  text string handed to Grounding-DINO. The walk-back, the projection, the
  assignment, and the export do not know what a pole is. Density is a
  property of the road, not the method: on roads where poles are sparse,
  expect the 82% regime today. Say "sparse in the frame," not "sparse class";
  poles on these routes are dense, which is exactly why they were the hard
  case to start with.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE D5 — Limitations
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- One route, one annotator: US 5 St Johnsbury, 56 poles, 763 pole-frames.
  VT 15/9 is onboarded and matched but not ground-truthed.
- The route is three capture sessions stitched under one label; continuity
  holds only within a session.
- The gate was tuned on the reported set. The sweep is flat from 200 to 300 px,
  so the optimism is small, but the number is in-sample.
- Right side, center camera, labeled poles only. FOV fitted from the data
  (19.5° half-angle), yaw assumed zero, frames beyond 18° discarded.
- Annotation completeness is unknown. Unlabeled poles are the main residual
  error and a major distractor source. Counts are a floor, not a census.

Speaker notes:
  Short and unapologetic. Every item here is also a hook on the next slide.
  Per-pole view, if asked: 47 of 56 poles have at least one correct frame;
  12 poles score F1 >= 0.8 and 14 score below 0.3. The tail is harsher than
  the per-frame average suggests.

SLIDE D6 — Next steps, in order of leverage
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Joint assignment: choose all of a frame's pairings together instead of
  left to right. One function; re-scores against the existing ground truth
  in seconds.
- Keep low-score boxes in the pool and add a box-height term to the cost.
  Height tracks 1/range (r = 0.80); near the vanishing point, height separates
  what column cannot.
- Temporal consistency: anchor each pole's identity in the frames where it is
  alone, then carry it through the crowded frames. The isolated frames become
  the mechanism, not just the diagnostic.
- Then: localize left-of-road poles (the XML already has them), use the
  forward-left and forward-right cameras with real intrinsics from VAIL,
  export a GIS layer with image evidence, and run condition assessment over
  the per-pole crops.

Speaker notes:
  The first item has already been tested offline against this same ground
  truth: replacing the greedy walk with an exact one-to-one alignment moves
  the full route from 57.8% to 66.0% F1 and leaves the isolated subset
  unchanged, which is exactly what you would expect if crowding is the only
  thing it fixes. It is not in the numbers on these slides; the reports for
  it are in final_report/reports_for_presentation/joint_assignment/.
  Why it works: 82 of the 270 wrong-box and missed errors were one pole
  taking its neighbor's box first. Another 52 had the true box hidden by the
  0.3 score filter before matching ever saw it. Both are policy choices.
  Temporal: 199 of 270 errors belong to poles with at least three correct
  frames elsewhere in their track, so an anchor exists for three quarters of
  them.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLIDE D7 — Summary
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- A geometric localization method that turns single-point pole labels into
  multi-frame visibility windows, with the camera calibrated from the data.
- A predict-score-assign-gate matcher with a reusable hand-built ground truth
  and offline tuning, so the answer key never has to be redone.
- A diagnosis: the remaining error is crowding, measured as a gradient, not a
  limit of the geometry or the detector.
- A deliverable that exists today: per-pole image folders and CSVs, review-
  ready for sparse poles and for any sparse, located roadside object.

Speaker notes:
  Close on the headline sentence. The project built the full loop: pipeline,
  ground truth, measurement, diagnosis, controlled experiment, and a measured
  path forward. Dense poles need the track and the box size, both of which
  the pipeline already computes and neither of which was in scope this
  semester. That is a scoping statement, not an apology.
